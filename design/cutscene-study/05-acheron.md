# 05 - Acheron Ultimate | Roblox VFX

- Video: "Acheron Ultimate | Roblox VFX", YouTube id `3KIzXAukn5I` (channel Divergent Vfx)
- Length 11.33 s (680 frames), 60 fps, 1280x720
- What it is: a Roblox Studio VFX showcase of a Honkai: Star Rail character's ultimate (Acheron). It is a katana iai
  finisher, not a fist fight. It goes: a close-up transformation, a petal wipe into a black/white world with water on
  the floor and a white arc in the sky, five or six dash-through slashes, a sheathe, a red crack web, two drawn impact
  frames, then the screen shatters and fades to white.
- Attacker: a blocky Roblox character with a custom anime face, long hair (blue, then white), a red box on her head
  and a long red katana.
- Target: a blocky avatar (green robe, pink hair, arms at its sides) that **never moves at any point**. It is a
  static test dummy.
- How I studied it: 6 overview sheets at 12 fps, 11 sheets at 30/60 fps, and one sheet of full-res crops around the
  target. Every frame was read at 60 fps for 3.45-3.70, 3.88-4.27, 4.78-6.37, 6.38-6.77, 7.08-7.57 and 8.93-9.72 s.
  1.47-2.43 s was read at 30 fps.
- What I could see: framing, cuts, camera moves, every screen effect, and the attacker's silhouette in the poses she
  holds.
- What I could NOT see:
  - The blade touching the target. Every contact is hidden by a smear, a blink or a flare, or falls between frames.
  - Any reaction from the target. It is a static dummy, so the video has no launch, no knockback and no hit-stop on a
    body.
  - Her arms during the dashes, which are hidden by hair, trails and blur.
  - What the red block is in 0.0-0.6 s and 7.2-7.35 s. It may be her hand, her sleeve or the sheath.
  - How the colour grade was built.

## Summary (5 lines)

1. This is not a punch reference. It is a katana iai finisher on a dummy that never reacts: no launch, no knockback,
   no hit-stop on a body, so F2 and F5 are "not shown" for bodies.
2. The dashes never show running. Dash 1: a petal puff where she stood, 9 empty frames while the camera pans and rolls
   after her, a 6-frame white smear passing close to the lens, then a crisp held pose past the target. Later dashes
   are 1-frame blinks that leave a red trail across the whole frame.
3. Wind-ups take 2-4 frames. She does them after the blink (6.00-6.05) or before she leaves (4.83-4.87), never during
   the travel. The swing itself is hidden under blur or a flare, and the next shot opens on the finished
   follow-through, held 0.35-0.57 s.
4. Contact is shown only by effects on the target's chest, on the side facing the camera, starting 0-6 frames after
   the pass. The kill comes on purpose about 1.9 s after the sheathe: a 1.5 s hold on the crack web, then 17 frames of
   drawn two-tone impact frames (9 red/black, then 8 white/black), then a shatter.
5. The smoothness comes from the effects and the camera, not the bodies. In the holds her pose is frozen while flares
   fade, petals drift and the camera keeps drifting. Camera moves carry the scene changes (fly-along, eased pull-up,
   charge-in and whip). I saw no positional shake.

## E. What it teaches us

**Strongest techniques**
1. **Show before and after, never the middle.** Every slash is a crisp held pose > 1-3 frames covered by
   blur/smear/flare > a crisp held follow-through pose. The eye fills in the swing.
2. **The trail is the travel.** A dash is sold by a departure puff, a full-length trail and a body smear, and the
   camera leads in the direction of travel (4.067-4.15).
3. **Effects carry the hit when the body can't.** The flare/flower is placed on the target's chest, on the side facing
   the camera, grows over 3-5 frames and lingers 15-35 frames.
4. **A fixed landmark behind the target** (the white arc) keeps you oriented across flips and cuts.
5. **A delayed payoff, earned.** The kill comes ~1.9 s after the action, but the gap has a trigger (the sheathe), a
   hold that keeps moving (the crack web spreads and slabs lift), a rising build (rays, a shrinking ring) and then a
   two-tone drawn release.
6. **Camera moves carry the scene changes:** fly-along, eased pull-up, charge-in plus whip. Hard cuts are few and sit
   on blur.
7. **A colour grade that keeps only the signature colour** makes every red effect read instantly.

**Principles to adopt**
- Hold end poses for 0.35-0.55 s and keep the camera drifting through every hold.
- Wind-ups of 3-4 frames, placed after arrival or before departure, never stretched across the travel.
- Hit effects start on the contact frame or up to 6 frames after it, and grow rather than flash.
- Impact frames as two drawn two-tone frames (one ~9 f, the other ~8 f) whose centre sits on the target and jumps
  slightly between frames.

**What would NOT carry over to an R6 blocky fist cutscene, and why**
- **Passing through the target and ending on the far side.** A blade cuts across; a fist must stop at the body. A
  haymaker that ends past the target reads as a miss.
- **The target's total stillness.** It works here because a cut "kills later". A punch is read through the target's
  body reacting, so a static target would look like a whiff.
- **A 9-frame vanish during the dash.** It suits a sword teleporter. For a brawler, the owner wants the dash shown,
  so keep the body visible as a smear.
- **The 1.5 s crack-web hold.** It only works with the sheathe as its trigger. After a punch, a still target for that
  long is exactly the "delayed knockback" problem.
- **Long hair and the cloth-like silhouette.** They give the held poses motion and shape; R6 blocks have no hair or
  cloth to add that overlap, so holds will look stiffer unless the camera and effects work harder.
- **The anime face close-ups** depend on a custom face texture.
- **The selective colour grade.** The method isn't visible, so treat it as unproven in Roblox.

**Concrete uses in our sequence**
- **Rush:** stop filming a held stride from behind.
  - Option 1, as at 4.017-4.267: a departure burst at the start spot, the camera panning and rolling toward the
    target, the runner crossing close to the lens as a stretched 4-6 frame smear, then landing crisp in the hook's
    wind-up pose.
  - Option 2: a side-on or 3/4-front camera with the runner crossing the frame left to right, plus a full-length
    trail.
- **Running hook and uppercut:** wind-up pose > 1-2 covered frames > held extended pose, with the impact effect on
  the target's chest on the side facing the camera.
- **Dash after the uppercut into the haymaker:** the arm is loaded before take-off (as at 3.95-4.00, where the sword
  already trails behind), or snaps back in 3-4 frames after landing (as at 6.00-6.05). No slow arm rotation during
  the rise. Carry the gap with a blur push (6.067-6.10) and open the next shot on the extended fist.
- **Final impact:** keep our drawn impact frames, but borrow the build: rays or a shrinking ring for ~10 frames, then
  two-tone frames of ~9 f + ~8 f centred on the fist's contact point.
- **Knockback:** if any hold sits between contact and flight, it needs something escalating on screen (as
  7.5-9.2 s). Otherwise start the body moving on the contact frame or within ~6 frames, matching the flare onsets
  here.
- **Orientation:** put a fixed landmark (light, pillar, sun disc) behind the target so cuts across the line stay
  readable.
