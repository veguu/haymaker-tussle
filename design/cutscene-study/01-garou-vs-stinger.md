# 01 - One Punch Man: Garou VS Stinger & Glasses

- **Video:** YouTube `pc7b7_unix8`, 15.5 s (931 frames), 60 fps upload, 1276 x 720. Fansub caption "Gigantic Drill Stinger!"
  is burned in from 0.0 to 2.7 s.
- **What it is:** TV anime (One Punch Man). Two exchanges. (1) Stinger charges Garou with a drill spear, Garou turns the
  drill aside, slips Stinger's punch, counters him in the face, then (after an abstract insert) juggles him in place with
  flowing arm circles. (2) Garou flashes in on Glasses, hits him in the chest, and Glasses flies away into a tree.
- **How it was read:** the 12 fps overview sheets, then every distinct image (duplicates dropped, source timestamps
  kept) in the rush, the drill pass, the counter, the whole juggle, the dash, the strike, the impact frames and the flight,
  plus full-resolution crops of the juggle, the counter contact, the strike and the flight. A per-frame difference pass
  over all 931 frames gave the cut times and the holds. Note: the upload is 60 fps, but a new image arrives at most every
  2nd frame (about 30 per second) and often only every 3rd to 7th. All frame counts below are **60 fps frames** (1 f =
  16.7 ms).
- **Could not see:** the launch that puts Stinger into the juggle (an abstract water insert covers it); Stinger's knockback
  after the counter (the shot cuts away while he is still falling); whether Stinger's toes touch the ground during the
  juggle (no shadow is drawn, and he stands a little behind Garou in depth); the exact depth distance of Glasses' flight
  (estimated from his size on screen). The pummelling of Glasses (8.98-11.28 s) was only read at 12 fps.

## Summary (5 lines)

1. At every contact the target's chest is square to the blow: Stinger runs face-first into the counter, is folded over
   Garou's rising arm in the juggle (belly down onto the strikes), and Glasses takes the strike in the chest while facing Garou.
2. The juggle never launches: Stinger hangs limp (toes pointed, legs dangling) folded over Garou's arm at shoulder height and
   is hit in place about 4 times per second, lifted only about 5% of frame height per hit. Garou stays rooted in a wide stance.
3. Knockback starts on the very next image (2 frames after contact) with no hit-stop. Glasses crosses about 1.1 body heights
   and leaves the frame in 13 frames. The impact frames come after he has gone, then the flight is shown again from the attacker's side.
4. The favourite result shot: Glasses flies straight away from the lens, front to camera, head bowed and arms and legs trailing
   toward the lens. He goes from filling the frame to 35% of frame height in about 0.6 s, stops dead against a tree at frame
   centre, and that pose is held for 0.33 s.
5. The rush is about 4 frames of horizontal colour smear, then Garou is already in the coiled lunge. There is no visible
   wind-up: the loaded pose drifts slowly for 0.47 s and then releases. Holds always keep something moving (speed lines,
   belt, blood, slow drift).

## C. Character animation and posing

**Stinger's charge (shots 2-3):** head lowered, brows down, mouth wide; shoulders hunched forward; the drill is carried in
front at shoulder height like a lance, with his body tucked behind it. In the head-on part only the drill tip and a sliver of
his hair show above it. The motion is carried by the drill's spin (its spiral lines change every image) and the rotating
background, not by limbs.

**Garou's drill deflection (shot 4, 2.73-3.05):** Garou turns from 3/4 front to side-on as the drill passes his right side.
His shoulder rolls back and his flowing left hand guides the drill head past him, with sparks where the hand meets the drill.
It is a turn of the torso away from the line, not a block.

**Slip and counter (shot 4 end, shot 6):** Garou leans and ducks toward screen right of the incoming fist. His head ends
past the fist, with his left shoulder and arm wrapped forward (4.23-4.45). After the black frame, the contact pose: Garou
upright, side-on to the camera, right arm fully extended forward with an open, water-wrapped hand under Stinger's jaw.
Stinger is caught mid-charge: chest square to Garou, head snapped back so the face points up, blood thrown upward, spear
arm flung out behind. Through the slow motion (4.73-5.57) Stinger arches further back. His head drops about 25% of frame
height, his legs swing up and forward, and his body tips from upright toward about 45°. He is falling backward over the
point of contact.

**Juggle (shot 8, 7.27-8.98):** see F1/F2. Garou: legs spread to well over twice shoulder width, knees deeply bent, feet
planted; he never steps. His upper body rocks and both arms make large circles: one rises from his hip up into Stinger's
belly (the blue arc), the other chambers at his chest. On hit images his arms are drawn only as the white swirl trail.
Stinger: jack-knifed over Garou's right arm and shoulder, hips the highest point (above Garou's head), head hanging
beside or behind Garou's head and face down, legs hanging limp with toes pointed. One arm still trails the spear across
the frame, and the spear tip swings with each hit. Each hit bumps him up about 5% of frame height (for example 7.98→8.02),
then he sags back. The animation is a reused cycle: 7.80/8.35, 7.88/8.42, 7.95/8.48 and 8.02/8.60 are the same drawings,
so the cycle is about 0.55 s with 2 hits.

**Glasses before the strike (shot 10):** classic boxer stance side-on, facing right, weight forward, fists at chin and
chest, rear leg extended. During Garou's 0.47 s drift he leans further in. He is fully square-on (profile) to Garou at contact.

**Garou's dash and strike:** no run cycle is shown. The dash is a horizontal smear of Garou's colours (skin and white, with
streak lines) entering from the right edge for 2 images (11.32, 11.35; about 4 f). At 11.38 he is in the final coiled pose:
very deep lunge, rear (right) leg straight and far behind, front knee bent, hips low, torso pitched forward, both arms
folded tight at his chest, head forward, mouth open. His yellow belt streams straight back, which is the only "speed" left
on him. From 11.38 to 11.85 he slides a little forward and the gap closes by about 30%; the belt flutters. At 11.88 the
contact image: his body is inside Glasses' guard, head at Glasses' chest height, arms still compact at his chest. A spiky
white burst of long comet strokes radiates from Glasses' torso, pointing left (the direction of the knockback). Follow-
through (12.10-12.22): same lunge, arms crossed in front of his chest, held.

**Glasses' knockback (shots 10-13):** in the side view he becomes a multi-image afterimage smear (several overlapping
copies), body tilted back, travelling flat to the left at torso height (no upward arc), and gone by 12.10. In the flight
from the attacker's side the pose is fixed: chest to the lens, head bowed forward so only his crown shows, arms flung out
to the sides and toward the lens, palms open, legs splayed wide with knees bent and shoe soles toward the lens. The torso
leads and the extremities trail. On the tree hit (13.00) his knees fold up and he sticks to the trunk, held for 20 f.

**Grounded vs airborne / root motion:** Garou is always grounded and rooted. His only travel is the smeared dash and a
small slide during the hold. Stinger in the juggle is held in place (no root travel) by the hits. Glasses' flight is a
straight, constant-pose body translation. It looks scripted rather than physical: no tumbling and no rotation.

**Transitions between attacks:** none are animated on screen. Every change of attack is a cut, a black frame, an insert, or
a smear. There is no dead time inside an action and no visible wasted rotation (the wind-up never appears).

## E. What it teaches us

**Strongest techniques**

1. **The target meets the blow square.** In every hit the target's front faces the attacker. The camera is placed so you see
   that: side-on for the decisive strike (shot 10), from the attacker's side for the result (shot 13).
2. **Knockback on the next image.** No pause before the body moves. The impact accent (grey/green frames) is inserted
   after the target has left frame, as a bridge to a second angle of the same flight.
3. **Show the result twice from two axes.** The first view is sideways (proves the hit and the direction); the second is
   down the flight line from the attacker's side (the body shrinking toward a stop point at frame centre). The cut between
   them is covered by impact frames.
4. **In-place juggle.** The target is not launched high. He hangs folded over the striking arm at shoulder height, limp,
   bounced about 5% of frame height per hit at about 4 hits per second. The attacker is rooted, and the arm arcs are big
   circles drawn as trails.
5. **A rush made from contrast:** empty frame → a few frames of smear → the attacker already arrived in the loaded pose. The
   wind-up is never animated; it is "already done" on arrival and then held with a slow drift.
6. **Slow motion that keeps moving.** After the counter, Stinger keeps arching and tipping with easing-out. Only one 7 f
   freeze, right at contact.
7. **Locked cameras plus one-image reframes** instead of long eased camera moves. The single smooth move (the push-in on
   Garou) is used during anticipation, not at impact.

**What would not translate to an R6 blocky cutscene, and why**

- Drawn smears, afterimage copies and stretched bodies: R6 parts are rigid, so a smear has to be faked (a stretched
  blurred billboard or a few ghost clones), and that rarely looks the same.
- Stinger's deep backward arch and the jack-knife fold: R6 has no waist or spine joint. The fold can only be posed by
  rotating the whole torso and swinging the legs at the hips (legs about 90° to the torso), which is enough for the
  juggle silhouette. The backward arch can only come from tilting the whole body.
- The 1.7 s abstract water insert: in a game cutscene it is dead time and breaks the player's view of their character.
- Heavy drawn manga speed lines and burst strokes: possible as screen-space UI overlays, but they replace backgrounds in
  the anime, and a 3D set behind them would show the cheat.
- Drawn impact frames (grey/green, silhouettes): only as pre-drawn image sequences (in line with our own impact-frame
  rule), not as a filter.
- Animation on 2s/3s with stepped camera pushes: the stepping is a 2D production artefact; in 3D at 60 fps it would read
  as lag. Copy the timing of the holds, not the stepping.
- Blood and gore: not for our game.

**Concrete uses in our sequence**

- **Rush:** stop filming the run from behind with a sliding held stride. Either (a) side-on locked camera, the runner as
  ≤ 5 frames of smear/ghost trail and then already in the coiled pose next to the target, or (b) head-on, the runner at the
  centre of radial speed lines with the camera retreating ahead and letting him gain (shot 3).
- **Running hook / final strike:** arrive already loaded (fist chambered at the chest or hip, deep lunge, rear leg straight,
  belt/cloth streaming back). Hold that for about 0.3-0.5 s with a slow forward slide (the gap closing about 30%), then strike.
  Remove the visible arm rotation entirely.
- **Flurry of rising punches:** keep the target low (folded over the attacker's arm, hips at about the attacker's head
  height, toes dangling) instead of high above. Use big circular arm trails, about 4 hits/s, a small bump per hit, and a
  whole-frame blur on each hit. Keep the attacker rooted in a wide stance. A reused 0.5 s cycle is fine.
- **Uppercut and the dash after it:** this reference doesn't show an uppercut launch. What it does show is that a decisive
  blow sends the target back and flat (not up), at about 5 body heights per second, out of frame in about 13 frames.
- **Knockback:** start the flight on the frame after contact. If we keep impact frames, put them after the target has
  started moving (as the bridge to a new angle), not before. If we keep slow motion, the target must keep drifting through
  it (no freeze longer than about 7 frames).
- **Result shot:** camera at the attacker's side on the knockback line. Target chest to the lens, head bowed toward the lens,
  arms and legs flung toward the lens. He flies straight away to a hard stop against an object at frame centre (about 0.6 s
  from lens to stop), held about 0.3 s with dust, then cut to a close reaction.

## F. The six questions

**1. Facing at contact.**
- *Counter on Stinger (4.55-4.62):* Stinger's chest is square to Garou. He ran face-first into the counter, and the camera,
  behind his right shoulder, sees his back. The blow lands on the front of his face/jaw from below (open hand under the
  chin) and drives his head back.
- *Juggle hits (7.27-8.95):* Stinger's front (belly and lower chest) faces down onto Garou's rising arm. The blows land on
  the front of his stomach from below, and he is folded around them. Garou's chest faces the camera at 3/4, and Stinger is
  seen side-on from his left. It is not a side hit: the blue arc always lands on his underside, not on his flank.
- *Strike on Glasses (11.88):* Glasses faces Garou square in profile (camera exactly side-on). The blow lands on his chest
  and stomach front. The flight shot confirms it: his chest (zip and collar) faces the attacker's side as he flies away (12.52 crop).
- *Drill pass (2.87-3.05):* the opposite case for a defender. Garou turns side-on so the drill passes along his side.

**2. Launch distance.**
- *Stinger:* the launch into the juggle is not shown (the water insert covers 5.58-7.27). During the juggle he does not
  travel: he stays at Garou's shoulder, bumped about 5% of frame height (about 0.07 of a body height) per hit.
- *Glasses:* straight back, flat, no upward arc. In the side view his leading edge goes from about 48% of frame width to the
  left edge in 13 f (11.88→12.10). That is about 1.1 body heights, still at full speed when he leaves (about 5 body heights
  per second). In the flight from the attacker's side (12.38-13.00, 37 f) he goes from filling the frame (chest at the lens)
  to full body at about 100% of frame height (12.63), then 35% at the tree (13.00). So he ends about 3x as far from the lens as
  at 12.63. Assuming a normal lens, that is roughly another 2-3 body heights (estimate). The size falls fastest at the start,
  as constant speed away from a lens would give, then he stops dead against the tree.
- *Set-up for the next attack:* here the flight is the finisher. The tree ends it, and a close reaction follows. The side
  view leaves the frame empty on the left with Garou holding his pose, a clean "they're gone" read.

**3. Rush readability.**
- *Garou's dash (11.32-11.38):* about 4 f of horizontal colour smear at body height entering from the right edge (about 30% of
  frame width), then he is simply there in the final pose. The camera is side-on and locked, perpendicular to the run. The
  read comes from before/after contrast (empty → smear → arrived and coiled) plus leftover motion on the body: the streaming
  belt and the trailing straight rear leg. Total about 6 f.
- *Stinger's charge (0.90-2.73):* head-on, on the run line in front of him. Subject centred at the vanishing point of a
  rotating spiral background, carried behind the spinning drill. The camera first races back (he shrinks from 55% to 11%
  of frame width in 20 f), then he gains slowly for about 1.2 s. It reads as unstoppable rather than fast, at 110 f.

**4. Dash into the final strike.**
- Garou arrives with the strike already loaded: arms folded tight at the chest, deep lunge. There is no wind-up during or
  after the travel and no visible arm rotation. The loaded pose is held with a slow slide for 28 f (11.38-11.85, a new image
  every 2-3 f, gap closing about 30%), then the release is one image: the burst at 11.88. The smear carries the travel and
  the held pose carries the transition. In exchange 1, the black frame (4 f) carries the move from the slip to the counter,
  and the counter is shown already at full extension.
