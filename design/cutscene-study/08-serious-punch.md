# 08 - "One Punch Man - Saitama's Serious Punch Sound Design"

- YouTube id: UwgyVQrlx_k. Length 17.2 s, 60 fps (1031 frames), 1280x720.
- What it is: an anime clip (One Punch Man: the attacker, Saitama, in yellow suit, red gloves and boots, white cape;
  the target, a huge grey-blue figure with purple armour), re-uploaded as a sound-design edit. Hand-drawn 2D, so the
  drawings are held for 2-5 video frames (animated on 2s/3s, about 12-24 drawings per second). All frame counts below
  are 60 fps video frames.
- What I could see: every frame of the punch (4.55-6.95 s at 30 and 60 fps), the contact at 60 fps with a 960 px
  zoom, the transitions after the hit at 60 fps, the build-up at 5 and 24 fps.
- What I could NOT see: the attacker's whole body during the punch (only head, shoulder, arm and glove pieces are
  ever on screen); the moment the fist touches the target (it cuts to an impact frame first); the target's torso at
  contact (only the face and a purple armour shape); any knockback, launch or rush (none is in this clip: the target
  is not shown travelling); what exactly the grey blob shapes after the hit are (they read as the target's flesh
  swelling, but nothing in frame confirms it); who the small silhouettes in the explosion and beam shots are. The
  6.0-6.7 s frames are dim and grey-blue, so colours there are uncertain.
- Sheets studied: the 9 overview sheets (12 fps) and 13 extra: 60 fps 480 px of 6.40-6.95 (contact), 30 fps 384 px
  of 4.55-6.40 (wind-up and arm travel), 5 fps 480 px of 0.85-2.65 (silhouette hold), 24 fps of 2.58-3.58
  (push-in), 20 fps of 6.88-8.38 (after the hit), 15 fps of 8.2-10.2 (payoff), 60 fps of 8.13-8.38 (flash) and
  9.40-9.90 (beam and graphic), a 960 px zoom of three contact drawings.

## Summary (5 lines)

1. The 6.5 s build-up is cut as long holds that get shorter: boots 0.75 s, a white-void silhouette 1.7 s, a push-in
   to the face 1.9 s, then a 4-frame "serious eyes" insert and 6-7 shots of only 0.15-0.35 s for the punch itself.
2. The fist never visibly winds up: it is shown cocked during the hold, then already thrusting straight forward. The
   one punch is replayed across 4 short shots from behind the attacker (5.30, 6.03, 6.27, 6.52 s), always as a
   straight push into depth carried by speed lines, smears and ghost copies, never an arm rotation.
3. Contact shot (6.517-6.667, 10 frames): low camera behind the attacker looking up past his arm at the target's
   face, which is front-on to the camera and the fist, mouth open. The touch itself is never shown: the next frame
   is an impact frame. The target's torso and the exact hit point are not visible.
4. Impact frames start on the very next frame after the last approach frame (0-frame delay): 2 black bullseye + 3
   two-tone silhouette + 5 radial-beam + 2 explosion frames (12 frames, 0.2 s), no shake. Then 32 frames of the
   target's flesh slowly swelling (moving, not frozen), then a 45-frame held follow-through with an easing pan.
5. No launch or body knockback: the payoff grows in scale instead (white flash 5 frames + dark 3 frames into a wide
   explosion, a vertical beam with a 3-frame tilt jolt, a 6-frame red graphic card, debris, a city-wide shot, fade).

- Anticipation: 0-4.9 s is all anticipation, built by holding the attacker still while something else moves (dust,
  cape, camera). It tightens in steps: feet, whole body in a void, face, eyes. The shots get shorter as it goes:
  0.75, 1.72, 1.9, then 0.18, 0.08, 0.05 s.
- Release: the 4-frame wide-eyed insert at 4.90 is the trigger. From there every shot is 0.13-0.47 s until contact.
- The punch itself takes 1.6 s of screen time (4.97-6.67) only because it is shown again and again from new angles,
  not because it is slowed down. Inside each shot the arm moves fast (the glove passes the head in about 14 frames).
- Contact to payoff: 12 impact frames, 32 frames of slow deformation, 45 frames of held follow-through, an 8-frame
  white/black flash, then the big wide shots (about 8.5 s of aftermath).
- Holds and hit-stop: the impact frames are each fully static (2, 3, 5, 2 frames), so the 12 frames act as a
  stepped hit-stop. There is no other freeze.
- Read time: the "serious" face reads in 4 frames because it contrasts so strongly with 1.1 s of calm face. Each
  arm shot reads in 15-28 frames because each one shows a single shape (glove, arm line, fist on face).

## B. Camera work

- Boots (0.17-0.90): ground level, close, behind/beside the feet; near-static.
- White void (0.92-2.63): wide, low-ish side view, the attacker faces right. The figure drifts from centre to lower
  left over 1.7 s at a slow, even pace while the shadow stretches (I can't separate camera move from figure move).
  No target in frame.
- Crouch to face (2.67-4.58): high 3/4 angle from front-left. Slow push-in (2.67-3.10), then an accelerating push
  (head width about 15%, 20%, 28%, 42%, 48% at 3.04, 3.17, 3.33, 3.42, 3.50) that stops hard at about 3.5 and holds
  for 1.1 s. It is drawn on 2s, so the push advances in steps every 5 video frames.
- 4.60-4.95: a glove wipe across the lens, then two step-ins on the same axis (closer face, then the eyes ECU).
- Punch shots (5.0-6.5): all from behind the attacker, close (shoulder, back of the head, along the arm), looking
  where the punch goes. The camera does not track the fist; the fist travels away from the camera into depth, or
  past it. Diagonal speed-line backgrounds suggest motion although the camera itself is static.
- Screen direction is not kept: the arm points LEFT at 5.30-5.57 and 7.45-8.20, but RIGHT/upper-right at 6.10-6.83.
  The cuts flip sides freely; graphic continuity (a yellow arm line with the red glove at its end) holds the shots
  together instead of the 180-degree line.
- Contact (6.517-6.667): low angle behind and below the attacker (his cape fills the top of frame), looking up past
  his arm at the target's face. The attacker is between the camera and the target, so the target faces the camera.
  The second drawing (6.600) is tighter: a cut-in or push of about 15% between drawings.
- Impact frames (6.683-6.867): no shake at all; frames inside each hold are pixel-identical.
- Follow-through (7.45-8.20): close, side-front of the attacker at arm level; the camera pans from the extended arm
  to his head. Movement slows down (his head enters at the right edge around 7.80 and settles about 10-15% of frame
  width further in by 8.15): an ease-out.
- Payoff: wide and static frames (explosion, beam, landscape); the only jolt is the 3-frame tilted beam (9.467-9.500).
- No FOV change, roll or dutch angle beyond the drawn compositions; the 13.83 landscape is a locked-off wide.

## C. Character animation and posing

- Build-up poses: standing tall with the cape billowing, then sinking slowly into a wide lunge facing the punch
  direction (legs wide, front knee bent, torso upright to slightly forward). Then a deep crouch with the head bowed
  and the cocked fist out to the side (red fist visible at the frame's left edge from 3.25 to 4.58). The fist is
  "loaded" from about 3.25 s and stays loaded through the 1.1 s face hold.
- Face acting carries the release: half-lidded calm eyes held 1.1 s, then the 4-frame wide-eyed, shadowed face. No
  body motion is needed to sell the switch.
- Body into the punch (4.97-5.30): the head whips away from the camera (smear, then the ear) and we see the back of
  the neck and shoulder with speed lines behind. That is the "turn toward the target", done in about 8 frames and
  hidden in a close-up.
- The arm: every time it is seen it is either already straight (5.30-5.57, 6.27-6.50, 6.517-6.667, 6.767-6.833,
  7.45-8.20) or moving straight forward past the camera (6.03-6.23). There is no shot of the arm pulling back,
  circling or rotating. The fist is shown with the knuckles forward, never turning on the way.
- Overlap and follow-through: the cape always trails (blown forward/up behind the arm in the contact and impact
  frames). After the hit the arm stays fully extended for 45+ frames (7.45-8.20) while the face is calm and only the
  strands, cape and camera move.
- Target: only seen in the contact shot. Face front-on to the camera/fist, mouth wide open (screaming) and stays
  open across both drawings; no visible recoil before the cut. After the cut, the grey blob shapes swell slowly
  (if that is the target's body, it deforms rather than flies). The target is never seen moving away.
- Grounded vs airborne: the attacker is on the ground in every shot where his legs show (0-3.1). No launch,
  airborne travel or physics is shown for either fighter.

## E. What it teaches us

Strongest techniques:
1. Holds that get shorter: two long holds (1.7 s, 1.1 s) with something moving in them, then a burst of very short
   shots. The contrast does the work.
2. The punch is replayed, not slowed: the same straight thrust shown 4 times from new angles behind the attacker
   (each 0.2-0.5 s) stretches a 0.2 s action into 1.6 s of screen time while every shot stays fast.
3. The wind-up is done before the travel and then hidden: the fist is cocked during the hold, and the
   cocked-to-extended move happens off screen, in a head-whip close-up, or as a straight pass by the camera.
4. Impact frames on the very next frame after the approach, then a short sequence of different two-tone/graphic
   frames (2/3/5/2), each held still. No shake.
5. After the hit, slow motion shows something MOVING slowly (swelling shapes, strands, a panning camera over a held
   pose), never a frozen screen.
6. A white wash (5 frames) then dark (3 frames) before the wide payoff shot.

What would NOT translate to Roblox R6 blocky characters:
- Flesh swelling, drawn smears, the head-whip smear and face acting (an R6 face is a decal, so an ECU on it reads
  flat; the "serious eyes" beat would need a decal swap or a light/colour change instead).
- Free screen-direction flips: they work here because the graphic arm-line matches across cuts. With small blocky
  bodies in 3D, flipping sides will likely confuse; keep the line.
- Drawing on 2s (stepped motion) at 60 fps would look like lag in a game camera.
- Multiple exposure can only be approximated (fading afterimage clones of the arm).

Concrete uses in our sequence:
- Lock-in close-up: accelerating push-in that stops hard on the face, then a ~1 s hold where the camera drifts
  slightly and particles/wind move (we have no cape or hair to flutter).
- Leap and haymaker: cock the arm BEFORE or at the start of the rise and keep it still while rising (no slow
  rotation on screen), or put the coil inside a cut. Show the final punch as a straight thrust from a camera behind
  the attacker looking along the arm at the target, possibly replayed over 2-3 short cuts (0.15-0.3 s each) instead
  of one slow shot.
- Facing at contact: put the camera low behind the attacker so the target's front faces both the camera and the fist.
- Impact: start the impact frames on the frame after the last approach frame, about 12 frames of 3-4 distinct
  two-tone frames, no shake.
- The 0.75 s slow phase: keep things moving slowly (target's parts pushed, debris, strands, an easing pan along the
  held arm) rather than near-still; then a 5-frame white + 3-frame dark flash into the wide knockback shot.

## F. The six questions

1. Facing at contact: only the contact shot (6.517-6.667) shows the target. Its face is front-on to the camera, and
   since the camera is low behind the attacker, also front-on to the attacker and his fist: square to the blow, not
   side-on. The torso is not visible (only a purple armour shape at left-centre behind the arm at 6.517, which I
   can't identify). The fist sits at the target's face height in screen space, but depth is unclear and the actual
   touch is cut away to the impact frame, so whether it lands on the face, chest or stomach is not shown.
2. Launch distance: not shown. Nothing is launched. The blast later rises as a vertical column off the top of frame
   (9.20-9.70) and fragments drift across the sky in the wide shot (13.83-15.0), but no body travels in view.
3. Rush readability: not shown (no rush). The nearest cue is 5.10-5.57: the camera behind the shoulder with
   diagonal speed-line backgrounds, with no legs or locomotion visible.
4. Dash into the final strike: there is no dash, but the wind-up-to-strike flow is clear. The fist is loaded (cocked
   at the side) from 3.25 and held through 1.1 s of stillness. The release has no visible wind-up: a 4-frame
   eyes insert, an 8-frame head-whip smear, then the arm is already straight or thrusting straight past the camera.
   The arm never visibly rotates. Cuts, a drawn smear (6.20-6.23) and a 20-frame multiple exposure (5.60-5.93) carry
   the transitions.
5. Knockback onset: no body knockback is shown. Contact to effect: the first impact frame (6.683) is the frame right
   after the last approach frame (6.667), a 0-frame delay. The 12 impact frames are each static (a stepped
   hit-stop). Then the slow phase (6.883-7.42, 32 frames) MOVES slowly: the grey shapes swell and sharpen. It is
   followed by the attacker's held follow-through with an easing pan (45 frames). The blast's big outward motion
   appears at 8.35, 1.67 s after contact, in a new wide shot.
6. Smoothness: every hold has motion inside it. 0.92-2.63: the cape billows constantly and the figure sinks into
   its stance. 3.50-4.58: the face is still, but the cape strands and the cocked fist shift. 7.45-8.20: the pose is
   held, and the camera eases along the arm while strands whip past. The camera moves ease (accelerating push with a
   hard stop at 3.5; ease-out pan at 7.45-8.20). The stiff parts (impact frames) are only 2-5 frames long. Constant
   cape/wind overlap stops the body from looking frozen.
