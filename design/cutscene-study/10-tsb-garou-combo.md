# 10. "unescapable garou combo" (The Strongest Battleground) - frame study

- Video: YouTube zP6MFM_SamU, 11.83 s (355 frames), 30 fps, 608 x 700 portrait.
- What it is: Roblox gameplay from The Strongest Battleground, Garou kit. R6-style blocky avatars. The normal player
  camera, not a cutscene. Attacker: purple hair, purple top, a rainbow-striped item on the back. Target "Monke_God":
  black outfit with a monkey-face graphic on the chest, glowing green mask; a long brown coat shows when airborne.
- The uploader overlays the input list at 10.08-11.83: dash, m1 x3, lethal whirlwind stream, hold jump, hunter's
  grasp, m1 x3, flowing water, m1 x4 downslam. The hotbar slot that lights up matches it (slot 2 about 2.08, slot 3
  about 3.33-4.0, slot 1 about 6.5-7.1).
- Could see: body positions, big poses, camera heading (from the floor grid), VFX timing, travel distance in body
  heights. Could not see: faces and hands (characters are only about 80-100 px tall), small hit sparks at 15 fps,
  whether camera turns come from the player's mouse or the game, and how high the target flies (the camera looks
  down, so height and distance mix).
- Method: 6 overview sheets at 12 fps, then 13 detail sheets (30 fps at every launch, dash, finisher and camera
  turn; 15 fps only for the first M1 string). Body height is used as the unit: one body is about 80-100 source px.

## Summary (5 lines)

1. The dash reads as a rush because of the setup, not the pose: one held stride pose for 10 frames, camera locked behind and above, the run going straight up-screen, a white ground ring at the start, a blue streak from the feet, the target growing ahead. The whole dash is 12 frames.
2. Dash into strike: there is no wind-up during the travel. At arrival the arm swings in 2 frames and lands on the 3rd (0.567 arrive, 0.667 contact). In the second dash (10.30-10.53) the arms are already forward and contact comes on arrival.
3. Knockback: on the Flowing Water finisher the target holds almost still for 9 frames (8.90-9.17) while a bright streak pointing the way it will fly pulses and the attacker keeps moving. Then the target tumbles away at about 0.3 body heights per frame. Other hits move the target in 0-1 frames.
4. Facing: at every judgeable contact the target's chest faces the attacker squarely (the monkey graphic shows to the camera). The camera stays behind the attacker, so the hit always lands on the chest front, seen past the attacker's back.
5. The finisher sends the target only about 3 body heights. The follow-up is the camera re-aiming at the target (10.0-10.3), then a 7-frame low lunge. Smoothness here comes from things that never stop moving (debris, smoke, trails, afterimages, camera drift), not from eased body animation.

## B. Camera work

One continuous gameplay shot. The camera is the player's camera.

- Shot size and angle: wide, high angle, from behind and above the attacker, looking down about 35-50° (the horizon
  sits at or above the top edge). The attacker fills about 1/7 of the frame height, at lower centre. The target is
  placed up-screen, beyond him.
- Position relative to both fighters: almost always on the attacker's back side. The attacker-to-target line runs
  straight up the screen. When that line moves (Flowing Water at 7.07, the finisher flight at 9.2, the downed target
  at 10.3), the camera yaws until the line is vertical again, with the attacker nearest the camera. The 180-degree
  line is not kept; it is rebuilt around the attacker every time.
- Movement:
  - Translation is a rigid follow with no lag, lead or easing. During the dash (0.233-0.533) the attacker's screen
    position does not change by more than a few px, and the ground ring shoots out of the bottom of the frame in
    3 frames.
  - Rotation is uneven: slow drifts (0.47-0.60, 8.00-8.60, 10.00-10.27) and fast whips ending in 1-frame jumps
    (see A). No overshoot or settle on the whips; they stop dead.
- FOV: no change visible. The attacker's size stays constant except when he is airborne (4.0-4.3).
- Roll: none.
- Shake:
  - The only displacement I could measure is a single vertical bump at the Hunter's Grasp explosion. The background
    drops about 3-5% of frame height over 4.40-4.47, then sits about 2% above the earlier level from 4.50.
  - I cannot tell a shake from the camera following the attacker's height change.
  - No shake measurable at the dash, the finisher (8.90-9.20, the attacker and floor grid steady) or the downslam
    (10.97-11.10, under about 1-2% of frame width if any).
- Camera vs impact timing: the turns happen before strikes (7.03-7.23 before the rush, 8.70-8.90 into the finisher,
  10.0-10.4 before the lunge, 10.6-10.87 before the downslam). During impacts the camera is still.
- Framing in motion: the attacker never moves on screen during travel. The world and the target move instead.
- Occlusion: the slab hides the fighters at 3.17-3.27 and smoke hides them at about 5.0-5.5. Gameplay accidents,
  not choices.

## C. Character animation and posing

- Stance before the dash (0.10): upright, back to camera, arms slightly out from the sides.
- Dash pose (0.233-0.533, held):
  - Torso upright; no forward lean visible from this high back angle.
  - Body yawed about 20-30° left, showing the back item.
  - Small split stance, one foot ahead.
  - Arms low at the sides.
  - No stride cycle: it slides. The 0.167-0.200 frames are the only change (left leg back, right arm out) as it
    snaps into the pose.
- Dash to M1: 0.567 arrive in the dash pose. 0.600 right arm out wide to the side, torso starting to turn. 0.633 arm
  sweeping across, body leaning left. 0.667 contact, other arm out for balance. A hook-like sweep made of 2
  in-between frames, with no pullback before it.
- M1 string (0.70-2.0):
  - Big, flat, readable shapes: both arms spread wide (about 1.0).
  - A deep low lean with the torso almost horizontal and legs splayed (1.13-1.47).
  - A leg pushed straight out low (1.53-1.80).
  - Each lasts 4-10 frames, with 1-2-frame snaps between.
- Whirlwind (2.30-2.87): crouch, stand and turn (2.50-2.57), then the target orbits him horizontally while he stays
  roughly in place.
- Hunter's Grasp (3.83-4.27):
  - The attacker throws the target up over his head (3.83).
  - The pair flip in the air with the attacker upside down below the target (4.00 and 4.13-4.17).
  - They come down with the attacker on top (4.20-4.27).
  - Airborne movement reads as physics plus animation, with flaring coat and tumbling limbs.
- Flowing Water rush (7.23-8.87):
  - Upright, fast alternating punches straight up-screen into the target's chest.
  - Afterimage copies hold earlier poses behind him, so the punching reads as a blur of arms.
- Finisher and recovery: arm toward the target (8.90), the other arm raised high (9.00), then he turns his back to
  camera and drops his arms (9.03-9.17). Follow-through runs while the target is frozen.
- Lunge (10.30-10.53): torso bent forward to near-horizontal toward the target, one leg trailing straight back, arms
  forward and down. Starts with 1 frame of lean (10.30) from standing (10.27).
- Downslam (10.967-11.0): from hunched over the target to standing upright over the crater in about 2 frames.
- Target reactions:
  - During M1s the target barely moves: an upright stun pose, chest to the attacker, a small head tilt at 0.73.
    This is the stiffest part of the clip.
  - Big moves throw the target's whole body at once: horizontal in 1 frame at 2.600, thrown overhead at 3.83,
    tumbling at 9.27.
  - During the finisher hold the target leans back slightly and does not move.
- Root motion vs physics, from evidence:
  - The dash and the lunge move the whole character with the camera (the ground scrolls past a fixed attacker).
  - Rubble chunks and slabs are physics objects; they fly, tumble and settle.
  - The finisher flight accelerates over the first frames and tumbles, which looks like physics, but I cannot prove
    it from video.

## E. What it teaches us

Strongest techniques:
1. A dash reads from context, not stride animation. Locked camera behind and above, run straight away from the
   lens, a ground ring left behind at the start, a streak from the feet, the target visible ahead and growing.
   Short: 12 frames.
2. The strike comes out of the travel at once: 2 in-between frames and contact on the 3rd, or contact on arrival
   with the arms already forward. No coil after the travel.
3. Afterimage copies behind a rushing attacker make a slow push (the ground scrolls only about 3 body heights per
   second) read as violently fast.
4. Knockback delay is made to read as power, not lag. The hold is short (9 frames). Something moves during it (a
   streak pointing the way the target will fly; the attacker's follow-through). The release is fast, with a tumble.
5. Always re-aim the camera so the next attack goes straight up-screen with the attacker nearest the lens. The
   viewer always knows where the next hit is going.

Principles to adopt:
- Never let the whole frame be still: debris, smoke, trails or a slow camera drift always move during holds.
- A held pose should be short and the change out of it fast (2-3 frames).
- A bigger hit means a bigger world-space event (dome, slab, column), not a longer freeze.

What would not translate to an R6 cutscene, and why:
- The 1-frame camera jumps and the ghosted frame (8.867): player-camera artifacts. In a cutscene they read as
  glitches.
- The rigid, unlagged follow: fine for play, flat for a film camera. A cutscene can afford easing that gameplay
  cannot.
- Slabs and smoke covering the fighters (3.17-3.27, about 5.0-5.5): accidental in play. In a cutscene they would
  hide the moment we want seen.
- The target's stiff stun pose during M1s: acceptable in play, where the viewer watches the attacker, but it is the
  stiffness the owner already dislikes.
- The 8-copy afterimage chain at full opacity: works at this distance. In a close-up it would bury the poses.
- R6 itself translates fully; these are the same blocky rigs.

Concrete uses in our sequence:
- Rush in: keep the behind-the-runner camera but make it rigid-close and short (about 12 frames, not a long slide).
  Add a ground ring at the launch point that is left behind, a streak from the feet, and keep the target in frame
  ahead and growing. Go straight into the hook with a 2-frame swing.
- Flurry: afterimage copies of the attacker (fading, 3-6 of them) make the punches read fast without speeding the
  bodies up.
- Uppercut to dash: before the dash, yaw the camera so the attacker-to-target line is straight up-screen. The
  reference's finisher travel is only about 3 body heights, but the reaction to it (camera re-aim, then a 7-frame
  low lunge) is what makes the follow-up read.
- Haymaker transition: the reference never rotates the arm during travel. Either the arm is already forward (lunge)
  or the swing is 2 frames at arrival. If ours must coil, do it before the leap, hold it loaded through the travel,
  and release in 2-3 frames. The reference hides its one awkward transition (rush to finisher) inside a fast camera
  turn.
- Knockback: cap the near-still hold at about 0.3 s, keep a directional streak and the attacker's follow-through
  moving during it, then launch fast (about 0.3 body heights per frame at onset) with a tumble.

## F. The six questions

1. Facing at contact.
   - First M1 (0.667): the target faces down-screen toward the attacker and camera, with the monkey chest graphic and
     mask visible. Chest square to the blow, hit on the front.
   - Rest of the M1 string (0.7-2.3, 5.93-6.9): the same.
   - Flowing Water rush (7.23-8.87): the target's chest faces straight down-screen into the attacker's punches;
     square.
   - Finisher (8.90): the target is three-quarter, front toward the attacker (lower-left of frame). The burst
     appears on that front side and the streak exits behind. Front hit, turned into the blow.
   - Whirlwind (2.60) and slams (4.20, 10.967): the target is horizontal or under the attacker, so facing cannot be
     judged.
   - The camera sees the contact past the attacker's back with the target's chest toward the lens.
2. Launch distance.
   - Finisher (8.90): the target holds 9 frames, then flies up-right and away, tumbling. It moves about 0.3 body
     heights in the first frame and about 1.3 body heights in the first 4 frames. It leaves the frame 16 frames
     after onset (9.75), partly because the target also goes away from the camera. It comes to rest about 3 body
     heights from the attacker (measured at 10.30, both at similar depth).
   - Height cannot be separated from distance in this high view. It is lying on the ground by 10.13.
   - Setup for the next attack: the attacker turns (9.75-10.27), the camera yaws to bring the target in, then a
     7-frame lunge closes the gap.
   - Hunter's Grasp launch (3.83): only about 1-1.5 body heights up, about 10 frames, then slammed straight down.
   - Downslam (11.0): the target is pushed about 0.5-1 body height into the crater, no flight.
3. Rush readability.
   - Pose: the opening dash (0.17-0.57, 12 frames) uses one held pose, not a run cycle.
   - It reads through:
     - The camera locked behind and above, the run direction straight away from the lens.
     - The 3-frame ground ring left behind.
     - Two blue foot streaks.
     - The target growing ahead.
     - Contrast: still for 4 frames before, a strike 3 frames after.
   - Flowing Water rush (7.23-8.87, 49 frames): reads through afterimage copies stacking behind, a foot streak,
     ground slabs and rubble scrolling under, and fast punching arms. The actual travel is slow.
   - No screen speed lines in either.
4. Dash into the final strike.
   - Opening dash: no wind-up during the travel; the pose is held unloaded. Arrival 0.567, arm swings out 0.600 and
     across 0.633, contact 0.667: 2 frames of visible arm rotation. Nothing hides it; it is just short.
   - Lunge (10.30-10.53): arms already forward, contact on arrival (10.53-10.57). No rotation at all.
   - Rush into the finisher (8.70-8.90): the change is carried by a fast camera turn and a ghosted frame. The strike
     pose is already extended at 8.90.
   - In none of the three does the arm coil during or after the travel.
5. Knockback onset.
   - Finisher: first contact 8.90. The target holds near-still for 9 frames (8.90-9.17), drifting under 0.3 body
     widths with a slight lean back. Visible knockback starts at 9.20, 10 frames after contact.
   - During the hold the body freezes (it does not move slowly), but the streak VFX pulses and the attacker follows
     through, so the frame is not dead.
   - No global hit-stop, no slow motion.
   - Every other blow moves the target at once: whirlwind (horizontal in 1 frame, 2.567 to 2.600), grab throw
     (3.83), downslam (displaced between 10.967 and 11.000). M1s have no knockback.
6. Smoothness.
   - Bodies are not eased much: a 10-frame held dash pose, a near-static target stun pose for about 1.3 s, a 9-frame
     target freeze. Pose changes snap in 1-3 frames.
   - The camera translation is rigid, and its rotations sometimes snap (5.73, 7.20, 8.83-8.90).
   - What keeps it lively: constant secondary motion (physics rubble always in the air, smoke drifting, trails,
     afterimages, floor rings), slow camera yaw drift between whips, and contrast (the attacker moves while the
     target holds).
   - Holds: the dash pose (the world scrolls), the M1 stun (the attacker's limbs move), the finisher freeze (the
     streak pulses, the attacker follows through), the end (the dust cloud drifts, slabs tilt).
