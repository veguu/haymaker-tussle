## Summary (5 lines)

1. F3 is the useful one: a 40-frame lightning dash filmed by a camera that tracks beside the runner and orbits from behind-left to his front, with the fist already loaded at the chest. No wind-up is shown.
2. The dash-to-punch join is hidden by a 3-frame star-flash cut and a 15-frame reaction shot of the target turning to face the camera in a guard. The attacker's arm then enters from the camera side already extending, and the blow lands on a chest and face that are square to it.
3. After contact the bodies freeze, but the picture never stops changing: pulsing radial lines, lightning flicker and a fading grade (0.6 s), then colour-glitch cards every 2-4 frames (0.3 s) and a black-on-yellow skull card (0.37 s). That makes 1.6 s from contact to launch without reading as lag.
4. The knockback starts on a cut at full speed: the target is already flying in frame 1 of a new static wide shot, crosses about 6 body heights to the lens in 9 frames and "cracks" the camera. A 2.2 s held end card follows, with the attacker still moving in the background.
5. F1, F2 and F4 are mostly self-display: strobing white silhouettes, music notes, a guitar spin and gameplay-camera lightning. The useful parts are F2's whole-body spin as a wind-up, its 1-frame blackout dash, and F1's 0.25 s strobe rhythm. Their full-screen VFX often hides the characters.

## E. What it teaches us

### Strongest techniques

1. **The fist is loaded during travel and the wind-up is never shown** (F3 17.80-18.43). The arm is held cocked at the
   chest while running. The join is a 3-frame flare cut plus a 15-frame target reaction shot, and the arm comes back
   on screen already moving forward.
2. **A reaction shot turns the target to face the blow** (F3 18.22-18.42). The target is seen from the side the
   attacker comes from and turns square-on into a guard just before the arm enters from the camera side. The fist
   then lands on the chest and face front.
3. **A dash camera that tracks and orbits** (F3 17.50-18.10). It stays close beside the runner, moving with him,
   and swings from behind-left to his front over 0.6 s. The background streams past, and he ends lunging at the lens.
4. **A frozen impact that never stops changing** (F3 18.78-20.07). The bodies freeze, but something on screen
   changes every 2-4 frames: pulsing radial lines, lightning flicker, a grade fade, then colour cards, then a skull
   card. The 1.6 s pause reads as impact, not delay.
5. **Launch on a cut at full speed** (F3 20.10). A new static wide shot along the flight line, with the target
   already moving in frame 1. It crosses about 6 body heights in 9 frames and ends on a gag (the lens crack).
6. **A held end card with life in it** (F3 20.32-22.50). The foreground is still; the attacker moves through poses
   in the background.
7. **A whole-body spin as the wind-up** (F2 9.42-10.22). The camera circles with him and a wind swirl carries the
   rotation, so no single limb rotates on its own.
8. **A dash drawn as a 1-frame state change** (F2 8.23). Same framing before and after, the grade crushed, a bright
   floor bar, and the runner ending as an outlined silhouette sliding to a stop.
9. **Every cut sits on a flash, flare or card** (all finishers). Camera jumps and 180-degree flips go unnoticed.

### Principles to adopt

- Show the target's front to the camera before the blow, and bring the fist in from the camera side.
- Keep the arm cocked through the travel; hide the last bit of loading in a cut.
- In a hold, freeze the bodies but keep 2-3 screen layers changing every few frames.
- Start the knockback at full speed on a new shot, not slowly inside the hold.
- Move the camera before contact, roll and push on contact, freeze after contact.

### What would not translate (R6 blocky cutscene)

- F1's strobe of white silhouettes with the target never on screen: it shows off the attacker but answers none of
  our contact problems. It would also bury R6 poses, which need clean silhouettes to read.
- F4's in-world gameplay-camera finisher: no framing control, and the VFX covers the bodies.
- Music notes, the guitar gag and the battery pack glint: character-specific.
- The lens-crack end card is a comic beat. It fits a "haymaker into the camera" idea, but it is a tone choice, not
  a technique.
- The colour-glitch cards cost little (ScreenGui images), but six colour swaps in 0.3 s may fight the two-tone
  impact-frame look we already use. Treat them as optional.

### Concrete uses in our sequence

- **Rush (owner: "reads strangely")**: replace the from-behind held-stride slide with F3's camera. Start tight
  behind-left and orbit round to his front over about 0.6 s while tracking at his speed. Pitch the torso forward with
  the fist cocked at the chest, flicker vertical speed streaks for 1-2 frames every ~12 frames, and open with a
  4-frame flash.
- **Dash to final strike (owner: "arm rotation awkward")**: stop rotating the arm on screen during the rise. Keep the
  haymaker arm already cocked through the leap, cut on a 2-3 frame flare (optionally 1 black frame) to a short
  reaction shot of the target (about 15 frames), then bring the arm in already extending from the camera side. If a
  visible wind-up is wanted, make it a whole-body spin or twist with the camera circling (F2), not an arm turning in
  place.
- **Torso facing (owner: "doesn't face the fist")**: in that reaction shot, turn the target square to the camera
  (12 frames is enough), and place the camera on the attacker's side so the fist enters between camera and target
  (F3 18.40-18.45).
- **Uppercut distance**: F3 doesn't show an uppercut. Its launch speed (about 6 body heights in 9 frames, from a
  static wide shot set along the flight line) is a model for "farther and faster". Then the leap after the target has
  real distance to cover.
- **Slow motion and knockback (owner: "delayed")**: our 0.75 s near-still hold can stay, but it needs F3's layers:
  radial lines pulsing every 2-4 frames, lightning flicker, a grade fade, then 2-3 card frames. Then start the
  knockback on a cut with the target already at full speed in frame 1, not easing out of the hold.
- **Stiffness**: add the 15-20 degree roll and small push at contact, slow pushes or arcs on every reaction shot, a
  decelerating pull-back on the final settle (F2 12.37-13.00), and background motion during the final hold (F3 end
  card).

## F. The six questions

1. **Facing at contact.**
   - F3 (only clear contact). At 18.40-18.42, the last clean frames, the target's chest and face are square to the
     camera, both gloves up in a guard. At 18.43-18.45 the attacker's sleeve/shoulder block enters between the
     camera and the target, from the lower right, driving straight in. So the torso is turned into the blow, chest
     square to the fist within roughly 20-30 degrees, and the hit lands on the front of the chest and face. The
     skull card (19.70) also shows the face front-on to the incoming block.
   - The target only got there by turning during a 12-frame reaction shot (18.22-18.42). It started 3/4 back.
   - F1: not shown. F2: the contact is inside a white-out (10.23-10.33); before it the target faced the attacker with
     its back to the camera (6.97-8.20). F4: not shown.
2. **Launch distance.**
   - F3, 20.10-20.25: from beside the attacker's fist to the camera lens, about 6 body heights. This is an estimate:
     the target is about 1/8 of frame height at the start, assuming a default-like FOV. It takes 9 frames to arrive,
     and it starts moving in frame 1 of the shot. It travels back from the punch (toward the camera) and slightly up
     (airborne, shadow separate). It sets up no next attack; the lens hit ends the finisher.
   - F2: the target ends far right on the floor; the travel is hidden. F4: the target ends about 3 body lengths from
     the centre, travel hidden (about 30.9). F1: not shown.
3. **Rush readability.**
   - F3, 17.43-18.10 (40 frames): torso pitched forward with the head below the shoulders, legs striding, fist
     cocked at the chest. The camera is close and tracking, orbiting from behind-left to his front, with the
     background sliding. Vertical speed streaks flicker for 1-2 frames, lightning wraps the body plus an orange
     swing arc. It opens with a 4-frame yellow flash and ends with him lunging at the lens.
   - F2, 8.23: a 1-frame state change, same framing before and after, grade crushed to black, a bright floor bar
     through the target and an outlined silhouette sliding to a stop (about 32 frames). The contrast with the calm
     wide just before (6.9-8.2) sells it.
   - F1, 1.50-2.33: gameplay run with a lightning trail only.
4. **Dash into the final strike.**
   - F3: the fist is already loaded during travel (cocked at the chest 17.80-18.07, raised by the head 18.08-18.10).
     No wind-up is shown after the travel and the arm never visibly rotates. A star flare (2 frames), a black frame
     (1) and a fading flare over the target (3) cover the join, then 12 frames of the target turning. The arm enters
     already moving forward at 18.43. From the end of the dash to the contact flash is 22 frames, none of them
     showing the wind-up.
   - F2: the wind-up is the whole-body spin (9.42-10.22, 48 frames) on camera, carried by a circling camera and a
     wind swirl, released as a throw.
5. **Knockback onset.**
   - F3: the target never visibly moves away inside the contact shots. 18.52-18.72 drifts only slightly, and
     18.78-19.38 is a true freeze (bodies still; only lines, lightning and grade move). Glitch cards (19.40-19.68)
     and the skull card (19.70-20.07) follow. The launch begins on the cut at 20.10 already at full speed: 98 frames
     after the first contact flash, 80 after the second. It is a freeze, not a slow-moving slow-motion phase.
   - F2: hidden by the white-out; the target is already down 11 frames later (10.42).
   - F4: hidden in the VFX.
6. **Smoothness.**
   - Camera: F3's dash orbit is one smooth, continuous move. Reaction shots get a slow push or arc (18.17-18.42).
     The contact gets a roll and push. F2's spin camera circles with the body, and its ending pull-back decelerates
     (12.37-13.00). F2's slide after the dash eases to a stop (8.27-8.80).
   - Bodies: the target turning during its reaction shot. Poses that commit fully (the profile freeze is one
     straight diagonal). Lightning and arcs overlapping the limbs during the run.
   - Holds and what moves in them:
     - F3 freeze 18.78-19.38: radial lines, lightning, chromatic rings, grade fade.
     - F3 skull card 19.70-19.98: lightning bits, then a white-out.
     - F3 end card 20.32-22.50: the attacker changes poses in the background, rain falls.
     - F2 glint 11.10-11.47: the flare shrinks.
     - F1 punch hold 6.67-6.80: lightning on the fist.
   - Every hold has something moving at a 2-4 frame rhythm. Every cut sits on a flash, so no camera pops.
